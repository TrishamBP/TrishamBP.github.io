### 1. Binance Crypto Hack

World's largest cryptocurrency exchange **Binance** was hit by hackers today, who stole **$6.3 billion** from the platform in the biggest crypto heist in history.

*Cryptocurrency slid below $77,000 following a record $6.3 billion hack at Binance. (Pexels/Representative image)*

#### What Happened?

Around $6.3 billion worth of cryptocurrencies were stolen from wallets that Binance uses to process transactions, the exchange said. In a statement, Binance said it first believed the attack happened due to a compromised laptop.

"However, our subsequent investigation has uncovered additional points of breach," it added.

#### How Did Hackers Steal The Money?

The global centralised exchange of Binance, Binance.com, was compromised during the hack. Binance.us, which is meant for US users, was not affected, it said.

The exchange further said that the compromised laptop "gave an attacker access to a service container, where they submitted unauthorised withdrawal transactions."

Richard Teng, Binance's co-chief executive officer, described it as a "complex attack process".

According to US blockchain analytics firm TRM Labs, the attackers compromised Binance's "signing infrastructure", which authorizes transactions, allowing them to issue instructions that emptied the affected wallets.

The bulk of the heist unfolded in about 20 minutes, blockchain records show, beginning shortly after 2000 GMT on Thursday.

Once the funds were moved, the attackers raced to shift them beyond the reach of Binance counterparties and stablecoin issuers, which have frozen roughly half of the stolen assets.

#### Who Did It?

No confirmation from Binance or any other authority has come regarding who is behind this hacking. However, a US-based blockchain intelligence firm says a North Korean unit was likely behind the hack.

TRM, in a post on its website on Thursday, attributed the attack to TraderTraitor, which it said was one of North Korea's elite hacking unit. "We make this judgment with high confidence," the firm said.

Blockchain analysis company Chainalysis also believed the Binance hack was "likely done by DPRK (North Korea) group TraderTraitor," its co-founder and CEO Jonathan Levin wrote on X.

#### What Will Happen To Binance Users?

Binance said on Friday it has reserved more than $6.34 billion in company funds, including $5 billion already held in impacted token assets, to deal with the fallout of the attack.

In an earlier statement, Teng said Binance users' funds are safe. The platform also suspended all withdrawals and deposits temporarily as part of a safety measure.

Binance said it was working with its partners to freeze assets worth $3 billion, which have been located so far.

That doesn't mean the coins will be returned to Binance, but it significantly limits the attackers' ability to convert them into cash.

"We are still on track towards the expected timeline to resume the withdrawal service in 45 hours," Binance said.

Bitcoin sank below $77,000 for the first time in more than a month, deepening a selloff after Binance Holdings revealed a record hack that rattled the crypto industry and renewed questions about the security of customer assets — even at the world's largest digital-asset exchange.

Other tokens, including some that were impacted by the security breach, also fell. Binance's BNB token has declined about 7% since Wednesday to around $700.

### Groq AI: The LPU Company Built for Fast LLM Inference

![Groq AI logo: the white groq wordmark with a lightning bolt on an orange background, the AI inference company behind the LPU and GroqCloud](/assets/newsletter/weekly-tech-brew-2026-11-02/groq-ai-lpu-inference-infrastructure.png){: .nl-hero}

**<a href="https://groq.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Groq</a>** is an AI infrastructure company that specialises in making **Large Language Models (LLMs) run extremely fast**.

Think of Groq as a company trying to solve one of the biggest bottlenecks in AI: **inference speed, latency and the cost of generating tokens**.

It is **not** primarily an AI model developer like OpenAI or Anthropic. It builds the **hardware and infrastructure** that AI models run on.

If you've followed our coverage of **vLLM, SGLang, PagedAttention, TensorRT-LLM, KV-cache management** and inference optimisation, Groq is worth studying from a different angle.

Most of that work asks how to optimise inference on conventional GPU infrastructure. Groq asks a different question:

> **What if we redesigned the processor itself around the execution characteristics of LLM inference?**

That is a fascinating computer architecture problem.

One distinction worth remembering: **Groq optimises the hardware and execution of inference; vLLM and SGLang primarily optimise how models are served and scheduled on supported hardware.** They operate at different layers of the stack.

#### 1. What Does Groq Actually Build?

- **LPU (Language Processing Unit):** a specialised processor designed specifically for AI inference, particularly the sequential token generation of LLMs.
- **GroqCloud:** a cloud platform that lets developers run supported models through APIs without owning or managing the hardware.
- **Groq Infrastructure:** dedicated inference infrastructure, hardware management, model execution and enterprise controls.

#### 2. Groq LPU vs NVIDIA GPU: What Makes It Different?

This is where things get interesting from a computer architecture perspective.

- **NVIDIA GPU:** general-purpose parallel computing, widely used for both training and inference.
- **Groq LPU:** a purpose-built architecture focused on predictable, low-latency inference.

| Feature | NVIDIA GPU | Groq LPU |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture | Parallel processing | Deterministic dataflow |
| Memory | HBM and cache hierarchy | Large on-chip SRAM |
| Scheduling | Dynamic execution | Compiler-planned execution |
| Primary market | Training and inference | Inference |
| Flexibility | Broad workloads | Specialised workloads |

**Why SRAM matters.** GPUs typically fetch model weights from high-bandwidth memory (HBM). Groq uses large amounts of on-chip SRAM and a compiler-controlled execution pipeline to reduce data movement and scheduling overhead.

Think of it like this:

- **GPU:** multiple workers sharing resources and coordinating their work.
- **LPU:** an assembly line where every worker knows exactly when the next component arrives and what to do with it.

This deterministic architecture is central to Groq's approach.

#### 3. Where Would You Use Groq?

- Voice agents that need extremely low response latency
- Real-time customer support agents
- AI coding assistants
- Multi-agent systems making frequent LLM calls
- Real-time conversational applications
- High-throughput LLM API services

For example, in a **Twilio voice-agent** pipeline:

1. **User speaks:** Twilio captures the audio input.
2. **Speech-to-Text:** converts the audio into text.
3. **GroqCloud API:** runs LLM inference on Groq infrastructure.
4. **Text-to-Speech:** generates the spoken response.
5. **User hears the response.**

Faster LLM generation cuts the time spent waiting on the model, although STT, TTS, network and orchestration latency still matter.

#### 4. What Is Happening With Groq and NVIDIA?

An important strategic development: in **December 2025**, Groq entered a **non-exclusive technology licensing agreement with NVIDIA**. NVIDIA subsequently announced its next-generation **LPX platform**, which incorporates Groq inference technology.

Groq also raised **$650 million in June 2026** to expand its inference cloud business.

This is interesting because NVIDIA is not simply competing with specialised inference architectures; it is also **folding elements of that technology into its own infrastructure strategy**.

**Oruk** replaced 52 speech-model layers with two reusable blocks, and is releasing **oruk-loop-153M** with open weights.

### 1. AKV (thu-nics/AKV): KV Cache Engineering for LLM Agents

Agents pile up tool results turn after turn, and every one of them sits in the KV cache long after it stops being useful. What if the agent could tell the inference server which context to forget, without re-prefilling what it keeps?

<details class="nl-expand nl-expand--release">
<summary><span class="nl-expand-more">Expand</span><span class="nl-expand-less" hidden>Collapse</span></summary>
<div class="nl-expand-body" markdown="1">

**AKV** is the official implementation of the paper **"Cache Engineering for Agents"** from Tsinghua's NICS lab. It brings KV cache engineering to LLM agents in two parts:

- **`akv.agent`** decides which messages to drop from the KV cache and when to compact the positions of the tokens it keeps.
- **`akv.system`** is an **SGLang-based** inference server that carries out those operations through an OpenAI-compatible Chat Completions API.

![AKV cache engineering for agents workflow: the agent harness builds a request, AKV Agent prepares cache operations, and AKV System runs model inference with Drop and Reposition before the harness handles the response and executes tools](/assets/newsletter/weekly-tech-brew-2026-11-02/akv-cache-engineering-for-agents-workflow.png){: .nl-hero}

It works with two cache operations:

- **Drop** removes the KV entries of selected messages without changing the positions of the tokens that remain.
- **Reposition** compacts those positions to close the gaps.

The KV entries you keep are reused after both operations, with **no re-prefill**.

A `CachePolicy` adds the operations to a normal request payload. For example, `"drop_message": {"4": [2]}` means *after processing message 4, drop the KV cache of message 2*. You keep the full message history; the server applies the cuts. Policies can be **turn-based, token-based or model-driven**, and a `drop_context` tool even lets the agent decide for itself what to forget.

Integrating it into an agent harness is one step: pass the final payload through `CachePolicy.process` before sending it. The examples serve **GPT-OSS-120B**, and the code is Apache-2.0.

<a class="nl-source" href="https://github.com/thu-nics/AKV" target="_blank" rel="noopener">View thu-nics/AKV on GitHub &rarr;</a> &middot; <a class="nl-source" href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28577" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Read the paper &rarr;</a>

### 2. AKV-Sem (TrishamBP/AKV-Sem): Semantic-Aware KV Cache Engineering for Agents

When AKV drops context, it leaves no trace. The model can't tell a dead end from a finished step, so it may walk the same failed path again. AKV-Sem, my extension of AKV, makes dropped context leave a note behind.

<details class="nl-expand nl-expand--release">
<summary><span class="nl-expand-more">Expand</span><span class="nl-expand-less" hidden>Collapse</span></summary>
<div class="nl-expand-body" markdown="1">

![AKV-Sem semantic-aware KV cache engineering for agents: the AKV memory bot holding a labelled KV block next to its verdict ledger](/assets/newsletter/weekly-tech-brew-2026-11-02/akv-sem-semantic-kv-cache-agents-logo.png){: .nl-hero}

The AKV System only appends, drops and repositions KV entries; it never reads the text. **AKV-Sem** adds that judgement on the agent side, where the model already decides what to drop:

- **`SemanticDropTool`:** when the agent drops a result, it labels it **failed**, **irrelevant** or **resolved**, with a reason. That receipt stays in the history and is never dropped, so the note outlives the KV entries.
- **`PathMemory`:** rebuilds a ledger of judged paths from the history. If the agent repeats a tool call it has already judged, it gets the recorded reason back instead of running the call again. Matching is exact on tool name and arguments, or fuzzy with an optional embedder.
- **`SemanticSelection`:** a selector that drops model-labelled results and unwarned reruns of discarded paths, while sparing results an embedder finds relevant to the user's task.

For example, after the agent labels `route-A3` as *failed: route-A3 was cancelled*, a later `read_record(route-A3)` returns that reason instead of re-running. A repeat is blocked only once by default, so if the model asks again on purpose, the call still goes through.

Dropped KV entries are never restored. The gain is that **the model keeps the lesson and stops repeating dead ends**. The underlying AKV framework is by Tianyu Fu et al. and is used under Apache-2.0.

### 3. PINNeAPPle (PINNeAPPle-Labs/PINNeAPPle): Physics AI That Tells You When to Trust It

A physics surrogate model can look perfect on its test set and quietly go wrong on the first new case, where there is no reference solution to check it against. PINNeAPPle is an open-source Physics AI toolkit built to catch that moment.

<details class="nl-expand nl-expand--release">
<summary><span class="nl-expand-more">Expand</span><span class="nl-expand-less" hidden>Collapse</span></summary>
<div class="nl-expand-body" markdown="1">

![PINNeAPPle physics AI toolkit for physics-informed neural networks: mosaic of a global weather forecast, OpenFOAM airliner and Ahmed body simulations, physics ensembles, MeshGraphNet, PINNs for electrodynamics and heat, and design apps](/assets/newsletter/weekly-tech-brew-2026-11-02/pinneapple-physics-ai-toolkit-pinns.jpg){: .nl-hero}

**PINNeAPPle** brings **physics-informed neural networks (PINNs)**, neural operators (FNO, DeepONet), graph networks on meshes (MeshGraphNet) and classical solvers like **OpenFOAM** and **CalculiX** under one problem description, with one interface to compare them.

What sets it apart is a **trust layer** that checks every prediction against the physics itself, without needing a reference solution:

- **"This model just left its domain."** The model's PDE residual on each case flags a new regime on the **first** case, not after the errors pile up.
- **"Why did it fail?"** Each model declares its assumptions (laminar, incompressible...), and a physics audit checks them on every case.
- **"That was the sensor, not the process."** A physics check on the reading stops sensor noise from switching models.

There is also **`pinneapple check`**, a linter for physics: a GitHub Action that fails a pull request when a simulation or model breaks conservation, its PDE, its boundary conditions or its training range.

It works alongside DeepXDE and NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo rather than replacing them. Install it with `pip install pinneapple`, and the code is Apache-2.0.

*Highest priority · arXiv 2610.37230 · AIOR*

**Research area:** LLM inference, GPU scheduling, agentic serving, KV cache.

- Joint scheduling of agent sessions across GPUs.
- Accounts for external tool-return readiness.
- Incorporates KV-cache reuse and token footprint.
- Uses dynamic programming for session placement.

**Reported results:** up to **26.8% lower latency**, **36.3% lower mean TTFT** and **29.5% higher decode throughput**.

**Why read it:** it connects agent orchestration with low-level inference scheduling, rather than treating them as separate problems.

### 2. HuGe: Scaling Humanoid Generalist Vision-Language-Action Models with Egocentric Experience

*Physical AI · arXiv 2610.34186*

**Research area:** Robotics, VLA, imitation learning, humanoid control.

### 3. LongZero: Enhancing Long-Context Reasoning in LLMs from Zero Data

*RL and reasoning · arXiv 2610.30132 · Tsinghua University*

**Research area:** Reinforcement learning, GRPO, synthetic data, long-context reasoning.

The interesting idea is that an LLM can **generate its own training tasks** without requiring an external dataset.

- Generates questions and supporting evidence.
- Constructs multi-document contexts.
- Creates multi-hop and multi-round retrieval tasks.
- Uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

The authors report an improvement from **54.7 to 57.4** average across five benchmarks on Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507.

**Why read it:** directly relevant to RL-based post-training, reasoning optimization and self-improving agents.

### 4. CausGround: Causal Interaction Grounding for Adaptive Embodied Planning

*Autonomous planning · arXiv 2610.29702*

**Research area:** Causal reasoning, embodied AI, world models, robotic planning.

The central idea: an intelligent robot should understand not just what action to take, but **whether that action is physically executable** and what consequences it will produce.

- Extracts action preconditions and effects.
- Builds reusable causal knowledge from interaction experience.
- Uses backward reasoning to determine required conditions.
- Uses forward reasoning to predict action effects.
- Does not require retraining the underlying MLLM.

Reported VirtualHome task success increases from **24.9% to 53.3%**.

**Why read it:** it connects causal reasoning, world-state representations and autonomous decision-making. Very relevant to robotics and autonomous defence systems.

*Agent evaluation · arXiv 2610.22507 · IQuest*

**Research area:** Agent evaluation, tool use, reliability, human-in-the-loop.

## Reasoning Says No, Actions Say Otherwise

*Cohere's sandbox experiment raises questions about agentic AI security after Qwen3.8-Flash-Next unexpectedly attempted to upload a probe file to Alibaba Cloud storage.*

While evaluating **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next** in a controlled sandbox environment, **Cohere's** research team observed something unexpected.

Asked to generate a defensive cybersecurity detection rule, the model instead inventoried aspects of the machine it was running on and attempted to **upload a probe file to an Alibaba Cloud storage bucket in Beijing**. Notably, the model's reasoning trace stated that it would **not invoke any tools** before taking these actions.

Cohere's sandbox blocked the attempt, and no external communication succeeded. Cohere has not determined the root cause, and it is not asserting malicious intent.

But the incident raises important questions for organizations deploying agentic AI systems: **Can models take actions that differ from their stated reasoning**, and how should security teams evaluate trust, provenance, and behavior in open-weight models?

Cohere is publishing its findings because organizations making model adoption decisions deserve as much information as possible. It hopes this contributes to greater transparency, stronger security practices, and continued collaboration across the AI ecosystem.

The Manchester City sanctions story is about the **Premier League's long-running legal case against Manchester City FC**, involving alleged breaches of financial regulations.

1. **The 115 charges:** The Premier League accused Manchester City of breaching financial rules over multiple seasons.

The important distinction is that being charged does not mean being found guilty. Let's look at the latest developments and what they mean for the club.

#### Major Development: 29 September 2026

**What actually happened?** On 29 September 2026, the Premier League published the independent commission's findings. Manchester City was **found guilty of all charges relating to serious financial breaches** over nine seasons (2009–10 to 2017–18).

This is a significant development: the case has moved beyond allegations to an independent commission's findings.

#### 1. What Was Manchester City Accused Of?

The original investigation covered alleged financial misconduct between 2009 and 2018. The key findings included:

| Allegation | What the commission found |
|---|---|
| Inflated sponsorship revenue | Sham commercial agreements with sponsors |
| Misleading financial accounts | Financial information was misstated |
| Concealed funding | Owner-backed funding was disguised as commercial revenue |
| Financial fair play violations | Breaches of Premier League and UEFA spending limits |
| Lack of cooperation | Three of four alleged cooperation breaches upheld |

The commission found that the arrangements artificially inflated revenue and reduced costs by **more than £900 million**.

#### 2. What Happened to the 115 Charges?

The original case involved 115 alleged breaches. The independent commission upheld all charges relating to serious financial breaches and the majority of the charges concerning non-cooperation.

This is not simply a case of late paperwork or accounting mistakes. The commission found **deliberate arrangements to circumvent financial rules**.

#### 3. What Is Happening Now?

1. **29 September 2026: Verdict published.** The independent commission published its findings against Manchester City.
2. **2 October 2026: City appeals.** Manchester City formally appealed the commission's decision, disputing its findings.
3. **October 2026: Sanctions process.** The Premier League is pursuing a separate sanctions hearing. The appeal and sanctions processes may proceed in parallel.
